## Part II — A voluntary philosophical statement

**Our direction is exploration in an endlessly open universe.**

We begin with finite lives, finite observations and finite means of expression.
Finite arithmetic truth gives us shared footholds: constructions, exact
relations and proofs that can be checked and developed further. From these
footholds, we seek new questions, new languages and new possibilities of life,
intelligence and creation.

**Unknown** names the openness of this undertaking. There is room for what
we have not discovered, what we cannot yet judge, and what we have not yet
learned how to ask. We offer our work to the public domain so that others can
take it in directions we cannot foresee. The undertaking can continue beyond
any one observer, generation, language or account of the world.

Here, "we" means those who voluntarily share these commitments. Receiving,
using or redistributing the Work does not make anyone a participant.

### 1. Explore the open universe

We wish to understand the universe's structure and changes, life and mind,
and our place within it, and to expand what can be created through that
understanding. We value curiosity and sustained, concrete work. Each discovery
can open further relations and questions. We leave the horizon of exploration
open to future experience and forms of understanding.

This openness is our philosophical orientation. The scope of any particular
mathematical proof or physical observation must still be established in its own
terms.

### 2. Find shared footholds in finite arithmetic truth

Our access to the world is finite. We seek dependable points of departure in
explicit constructions, exact arithmetic relations and checkable reasoning.
We state what a claim concerns, the assumptions it uses and how it is checked,
so another finite observer can understand, challenge and continue the work.

A finite proof can establish a claim about infinitely many instances when its
reasoning justifies that scope. Finite observations also have their own scope;
extending a conclusion requires an account of why those observations suffice.
The limits of an observer's knowledge do not make truth a matter of preference.

### 3. Preserve the unknown and continue inquiry

When evidence is insufficient, we record Unknown honestly. Exhausting a finite
search leaves an unresolved question; a claim of impossibility needs a proof
and an explicit scope. We retain what remains unsettled so it can still be
recognized, reformulated and investigated.

The finite observer's logic remains open to further questions, distinctions
and encounters with the world. A preserved unknown gives a later inquiry a
place to begin.

### 4. Create room for new questions and languages

We leave room for future discoveries, forms of life and intelligence, and ways
of understanding. Those who follow may ask questions not yet formed, revise
our answers and revise our ways of asking. Every generation's achievements
should leave space for its successors to create.

We can develop the languages, methods and tools through which questions become
expressible. We retain the connections and differences between old and new
forms, so that extending a language also leaves a path for understanding it.

### 5. Let imagination face testing

We welcome bold conjectures and take counterexamples seriously. Intuition,
hypothesis, observation and proof should be clearly distinguished. Conclusions
should be traceable to their grounds and checks. Corrections should leave an
identifiable record, and unresolved questions should be retained alongside
achievements.

### 6. Continue a long undertaking through finite actions

Each investigation should state its question, assumptions, checking method,
resource limits and exit conditions. At its end, retain what was gained, what
failed and what remains unresolved. A further attempt starts from an explicit
continuation point and states its conditions and costs anew.

We wish the undertaking to outlast any individual, while respecting each
participant's finite life, resources and choices.

### 7. Cooperate with freedom and responsibility

Participants may question, decline endorsement, leave, or keep a different
research branch. Trust should support candid correction and leave disagreements
open to understanding and continuation.

We record human intentions, machine-generated material and other contributions
honestly, distinguishing drafting, checking and adoption. Attribution records
contributions; accounts record submission channels. Correctness still requires
appropriate evidence. Each participant answers for their actual involvement
and explicit commitments. These are voluntary working practices, not copyright
conditions on downstream users.

### 8. Care for the world that supports exploration

Exploration also depends on the lives, societies and shared world around us.
We consider the effects of our actions on others and the environment, respect
participants' choices, treat consequences that are hard to undo with care,
and seek suitable ways for more people to benefit from our work.

### 9. Allow this statement to develop

This statement also comes from limited understanding. It may be questioned,
revised and voluntarily adopted again. Revisions should retain their versions,
reasons and disagreements, so successors can understand how commitments formed
and why they changed. Such revisions do not reduce the rights in Part I.

We wish to leave reliable work and honest unknowns.

In this spirit, we wish to explore the universe.
